How this was derived

Facing is the CDIP MOP shore normal at the transect the nearshore forecast uses for the spot, averaged over about ±200 m of coast. Offshore wind comes from facing + 180°. Swell window is the open water within ±90° of facing (rays every 2° over the Salix elevation grids; only land within 30 km blocks; gaps up to 8° bridged).

Bottom type: point if the coast bends seaward by 20° or more within ±500 m; otherwise reef if at least 40% of the transect is hard seabed in CDFW's Predicted Nearshore Benthic Substrates of California (BIOS ds3091, CC BY 4.0), or at least 25% with a mostly rocky shoreline in CDFW's Shoreline Types (BIOS ds3115, NOAA ESI, CC BY 4.0); otherwise beach. Size: every spot peaks at 1.8 m; steep or gravel beaches close out sooner.

Label scales. Adopted (2026-10-08): Very poor 0, Poor 5, Poor to fair 8, Fair 28, Fair to good 48 (fitted at 42, raised to keep the top label rare), capped there; Good and Epic reserved for people (human forecasts or observations) or a model calibrated on user reports. Previous: Poor 0, Fair 22, Good 42, Excellent 70, Epic 92. Surfline's 7 ratings mapped onto the same score: Very poor below 5, Poor 5, Poor to fair 8, Fair 28, Fair to good 42 (fitted), then Good 70 and Epic 92 (set to match ours). In the capped version, as on Surfline, the model stops at Fair to good and the top two are reserved for observed reports. Ours 7 uses Surfline's steps but tops out at Excellent instead of Epic.

Anything entered in the rating columns of locations.csv overrides these values. Tide is not part of the hourly score yet.
